# Career & Positioning — Rules

Confirmed principles — apply by default. Demote to a hypothesis if contradicted by new data.

## R1: Optimize for freedom + meaning + "enough", not max wealth or control

The objective function is Barista-FIRE-funded freedom to do valuable/research work while being a
present parent. Drop founder/equity-maximization pressure; it doesn't serve the owner's stated goals.

## R2: Financial resilience funds the research dream — sequence, don't trade off

Barista-FIRE (#3) is the engine for "recognized researcher" (#1). Protect the savings rate, avoid
lifestyle creep (it erodes the US-salary/Poland-cost arbitrage that powers FIRE). Reaching "enough"
is what makes the research freedom real.

## R3: Redefine "recognized researcher" as niche bridge-builder, not frontier inventor

Aim to be the recognized applied-research voice connecting RDL / tabular FMs to real relational
field-sales data. Use the unique asset (production data + live testbed) academics lack. Frontier
research is off-strategy given the parenting + FIRE + solo constraints.

## R4: Sustainable cadence is a hard constraint (newborn-compatible)

~1 solid public artifact per quarter, built as a *side-stream*, never a grind. Let the day job's
problems *be* the research material (the writeup is the generalized distillation of work already
done). A slipped quarter is fine — FIRE doesn't depend on the research, so there's no financial
pressure forcing a grind.

## R5: Sequence the job trust → infra → ambitious models

Earn trust with a simple, rigorously-evaluated next-best-action baseline (gradient-boosted trees) and
great data/eval infrastructure **first**. Introduce RDL / tabular FMs only once the eval harness can
prove they beat the baseline. Over-engineering early is the #1 way to lose ML-ownership credibility.

## R6: Invest in the ends (specification + verification) and in orchestration, not in typing code

Code generation is commoditizing. Be a **director + verifier** of AI: master context engineering
(`AGENTS.md`/rules + tight specs), the plan→execute→verify loop against eval/test harnesses you own,
and parallel/background/cloud agents. "Prompt cleverness" is a feature, not a moat.

## R7: Automate what's verifiable; keep what requires judgment

Delegate to agents: data pipelines, schema exploration, feature-engineering boilerplate, dbt/IaC,
docs, test generation. Keep for yourself: model selection, eval design, what the model should
optimize, interpreting results, money-/privacy-/security-sensitive correctness. Knowing the boundary
is the senior skill.

## R8: Make the codebase agent-legible early

Strong types, a test suite, one-command dev setup, modular boundaries, an `AGENTS.md`. Agents perform
far better in legible repos; the investment compounds on every future run and doubles as trust-building
engineering hygiene. (This repo already models the pattern.)

## R9: Negotiate publishing rights + salary, not equity

Given the owner's goals, the valuable levers are the right to publish methods / open-source
non-proprietary tooling (enables #1) and salary (powers FIRE). Equity matters little because the
founder/ownership path is deprioritized.

## R10: Eval-driven development is the triple-duty skill — prioritize it

Rigorous evals/measurement simultaneously: verify agent-written code, build trust at the job, and
provide research rigor. Same theme as the repo's OPE-gated promotion discipline. Invest here above
modeling flash.

## R11: Guard against solo-ML burnout; push for a team at the ROI proof point

Solo ML + data engineering + newborn is a real burnout risk. Automate the DE load hard, clarify scope,
and push for headcount the moment ROI justifies it — leading a team buys back time (direct, don't
implement) and protects the parenting constraint.

## R12: Re-evaluate the stack ~quarterly, not weekly

Meta-skills (specification, verification, orchestration, domain depth) transfer across tools; the
model/UI-of-the-month does not. Optimize for speed of re-learning, not mastery of any current stack.
